pre-release

Every music app treats your library as a list. Legato treats it as a graph.

A local-first player where every recording, artist, album, and label gets its own wiki-style article — dense with links to everything else in your collection. Read about a track and it teaches you something: the engineer also worked on three other records you own, or two artists you never connected share a producer.

the canvas

Browse by association, not by search

There's no folder tree and no flat list of ten thousand tracks. The canvas is a live, force-directed graph of your library — recordings, artists, releases, labels, producers — laid out by how they connect to each other. Pan into a cluster and you're exploring the shape of your own taste.

the article

Every record has something to teach you

Click a node and it opens like an encyclopedia entry, not a details pane. Releases, recordings, credits — every fact links somewhere else in your collection, so reading about a record is how you find the connections you didn't know were there.

See the shape of your taste

Collection understanding: clusters, gaps, and the corners of your library that are quietly over-represented. The graph makes the shape of what you own visible — size alone doesn't show it.

Fix your metadata in place

Library hygiene: Legato surfaces missing, inconsistent, or conflicting tags and lets you fix them right there on the graph — no separate tagging app, no round trip through a spreadsheet.

A plain library when you want one

Some nights you just want the album. Next to the map sits an ordinary album grid and track table, fast at thirty thousand albums, sharing the same search and selection. Switch between them and nothing gets lost.

Ink or paper

The dark theme you see here, plus a light one modelled on sheet music: warm off-white paper and pen-ink black, with every colour checked for contrast. Pick one or let it follow your system.

ways in

Start wherever your music already lives

on a home server

For the NAS in the closet

Docker, Synology Container Manager, Unraid, or a one-line install on Linux. First boot shows a short claim code, and after that every device on your network opens Legato in a browser, no desktop session required. Built to scan six-figure FLAC libraries, with progress you can pause, resume, and trust.

on your computer

For the library on your laptop

A signed desktop app for Mac (Apple Silicon and Intel), Windows, and Linux. It finds your music folders, plays gaplessly through native audio, and can keep serving your library from the menu bar after you close the window, so your phone still reaches it.

in a browser

For people who own no files at all

A free legato.fm account turns your Spotify playlists into a map of your taste. Play tracks through a friend's Legato server when they invite you, or hand them off to Spotify. Nothing to install, and running your own library stays an option for later.

On your phone, Legato installs from the browser at launch, with lock-screen controls. Native mobile apps follow.
